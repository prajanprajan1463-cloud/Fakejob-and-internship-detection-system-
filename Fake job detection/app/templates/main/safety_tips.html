Student & Job Seeker Defense

Job Search Safety Guidelines

Critical rules and best practices to protect your finances, privacy, and personal data while searching for employment and internship opportunities.

1. Legitimate Employers Never Charge Candidates
Zero upfront payment policy

Under no circumstances should you ever pay for a job, training materials, onboarding security deposits, application processing, or laptop delivery. Reputable companies cover their own hiring overhead and equipment costs.

If an employer demands payment via Western Union, gift cards, UPI, or crypto, cease communication immediately.
2. Protect OTPs, PINs & Passwords
Credential Phishing

Employers do not need access to your online banking, credit card number, or OTPs. Direct deposit routing details are only submitted after an official signed contract and via secure employee onboarding portals.

Never read aloud or screenshot two-factor authentication SMS codes sent to your phone.
3. Inspect Recruiter Email Domains
Domain Spoofing & Lookalikes

Verify the sender's domain (the part after the @). Legitimate corporate talent teams email from recruiter@company.com, not free providers like company.recruitment@gmail.com or typosquatted domains (e.g., micros0ft-careers.com).

Check the official company domain on WHOIS to confirm how long the domain has existed.
4. Beware of Unrealistic Compensation
Advance-Fee Bait

Offers promising exorbitant sums (such as $4,000/week or ₹50,000/day) for low-skilled tasks like data entry, watching YouTube videos, or typing copy are standard advance-fee or task-based scam bait.

Research baseline market salaries for your region and role on Glassdoor and Levels.fyi.
5. Insist on Structured Interviews
Anonymous Messenger Fraud

Genuine hiring involves live two-way dialogue—in person or via verified video conferencing (Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams). Avoid employers whose entire "interview" consists of anonymous Telegram questionnaires.

Confirm the interviewer's identity on LinkedIn and ensure their listed employer matches.
6. Refuse "Equipment Check" Requests
Fake Check Deposit Fraud

Scammers send a counterfeit check, instruct you to deposit it, keep an "advance", and forward the rest to their "supplier" for work laptops. When the check bounces 1-2 weeks later, your bank holds you responsible for the entire sum.

Never deposit checks from unfamiliar third parties into your personal bank account.
