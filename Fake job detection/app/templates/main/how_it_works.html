Technical Overview

How The Detection Engine Works

A dual-engine approach uniting rule-based heuristic patterns with machine learning text classification for transparent, explainable threat scoring.

1. Text Normalization

Strips HTML injection, normalizes Unicode character sets, removes extraneous whitespaces, and normalizes contact handles and links for tokenization.

2. Heuristic Rules

Evaluates regex signatures targeting advance fees, fake cashier checks, cryptocurrency payments, Telegram/WhatsApp-only screening, and urgency tactics.

3. Scikit-learn TF-IDF

Converts vocabulary into n-gram TF-IDF vectors (1-2 grams) evaluated through a calibrated Logistic Regression classifier to calculate probability of fraud.

4. Score Synthesis

Fuses heuristic evidence (60%) and ML prediction (40%), bounds score to 0–100, enforces safety floors for critical red flags, and extracts evidence.

Scoring Methodology & Thresholds

To prevent false sense of security or runaway penalties, our multi-factor scoring algorithm applies diminishing marginal penalties to cumulative red flags while enforcing non-negotiable safety floors for confirmed advance-fee demands.

0 – 29 Points
Lower Risk Indicators

Posting conforms to standard recruitment standards. No overt scam keywords or payment solicitations identified.

30 – 59 Points
Needs Further Verification

Contains cautionary indicators such as free webmail recruitment, chat-only communications, or unusually high pay for entry-level work.

60 – 100 Points
Potentially Suspicious

Contains critical fraud indicators such as upfront fee requests, cashier's check equipment schemes, or bank credential harvesting.

Academic Project & Verification Notice

This project was engineered for an undergraduate BCA capstone demonstration. While the detection engine implements verified heuristic signatures and machine learning models, no automated system can guarantee 100% scam detection or business verification.

Job seekers should always cross-reference openings directly on official corporate portals, inspect corporate domain registration records, and report suspicious communications to cybercrime authorities.
